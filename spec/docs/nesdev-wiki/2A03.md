# 2A03

> Source: <https://www.nesdev.org/wiki/2A03> — NESdev Wiki, revision [21333](https://www.nesdev.org/w/index.php?oldid=21333) (last edited 2023-10-08T20:34:27Z), retrieved 2026-10-06. Authors: NESdev Wiki contributors ([history](https://www.nesdev.org/w/index.php?title=2A03&action=history)). Images omitted. See [SOURCES.md](../SOURCES.md) for license.

The 2A03, short for RP2A03, is the common name of the NTSC [NES CPU](CPU.md) chip. It consists of a MOS Technology 6502 processor (lacking decimal mode) and audio, joypad, and DMA functionality. PAL systems use the similar RP2A07, which has a different clock rate, adjusted sampled audio rates, and DMA bugfixes.

## 2A03 register map

In addition to the registers in the 6502 core, the 2A03 contains 22 memory-mapped registers for sound generation (see [NES APU](APU.md)), joystick reading, and OAM DMA transferring. Unlike the addresses of [PPU](PPU.md) registers and [mapper](Mapper.md) registers, CPU register addresses are completely decoded, which means that the entire space from the end of CPU registers to the top of address space ($4020 through $FFFF) is available to the Game Pak.

The range $4018-$401F does nothing on a retail NES. It was intended for 2A03 functionality that never made it to production. Various revisions of the 2A03 include [test registers](https://www.nesdev.org/wiki/CPU_Test_Mode) (which are disabled in normal operation) or [remnants of an incompletely implemented IRQ counter](https://www.nesdev.org/wiki/RP2A03_Programmable_Interval_Timer) that was disconnected from the rest of the circuit. Mappers can place writable registers here without conflicting with the 2A03, but placing readable registers here should be avoided because of [conflicts with DMA](DMA.md#Register_conflicts).
